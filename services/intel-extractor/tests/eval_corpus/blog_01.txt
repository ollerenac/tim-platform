Inside StoneLoader: How a Commodity Loader Grew Up

By the Helios Labs Threat Research Team

When we first saw StoneLoader in late 2025 it was, frankly, boring: a two-stage loader with
hardcoded infrastructure and no obfuscation to speak of. Six months later it has become the
delivery vehicle of choice for at least three distinct intrusion sets, and the latest builds
show a level of operational discipline that deserves attention.

The most interesting change is in how StoneLoader resolves its command-and-control. Early
builds embedded raw addresses in the binary. Current builds compute a per-victim beacon
schedule and fetch their tasking from a metrics-themed domain, stone-metrics.example, that
blends neatly into the noise of legitimate telemetry traffic most enterprises generate. If
you are hunting, DNS is your friend here: the domain has no legitimate business appearing in
your logs.

We also observed the operators experimenting with signed drivers to blind endpoint sensors,
a technique that has been trickling down from ransomware crews for a year now. The sample we
analyzed in depth carries the SHA-256 hash 4c7bf192284983cdb53f47f5f059861bd601e68a900004c7c2d5b1edcf9462ac and was compiled
less than 48 hours before we caught it in a customer environment, which tells you something
about how short the shelf life of these builds is.

Our full technical teardown, including the beacon scheduling algorithm and YARA rules, is
available to Helios Labs subscribers. The short version for defenders: watch your DNS, watch
for drivers loaded from user-writable paths, and assume any loader infection is thirty
minutes away from becoming a hands-on-keyboard intrusion.
