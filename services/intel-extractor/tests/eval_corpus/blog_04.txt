TidePump: A Cryptominer That Really Wants to Be a Botnet

By Helios Labs Threat Research

Most cryptominers are smash-and-grab operations. TidePump is trying to be something more,
and watching it evolve has been one of the stranger pleasures of this quarter.

The initial infection is unremarkable: a trojanized system optimization tool, pushed through
malvertising, with the installer hash e8f98c08e9501fa2fbfc94ee77123264 (MD5). What happens next is not. Instead of
immediately mining, TidePump inventories the host, estimates its value, and phones home for
instructions. High-value hosts, those with GPUs or with credentials cached for cloud
consoles, are held back from mining entirely and cataloged for what we assess is resale to
other operators.

The update channel lives at tidepump-updates.example and new modules are fetched from
https://tidepump-updates.example/get with a hardcoded user agent that makes for an easy
network detection while it lasts. We counted four module updates in five weeks, which is a
faster development cadence than some commercial software we could name.

The takeaway: the line between commodity crimeware and access brokerage keeps blurring. A
miner infection is no longer a nuisance to clean up on a quiet Friday. It is a signal that
your host is being evaluated by someone with a business model, and you should respond to it
with the urgency that implies.
